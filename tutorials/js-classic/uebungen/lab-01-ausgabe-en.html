Lab 01 — First Output

Build your first page about you. Three files, three outputs, one success. Time: about 30 min.

Your task

You build your first own web page. The page is about you. It has three files and three JavaScript outputs.

  1. Your page has three files. The files are called index.html, css/style.css and js/main.js. All three files live in one folder. All three are linked.
  2. Your page greets the visitor. The greeting is on the page. It sits in an element with id="gruss". JavaScript writes the greeting into the element with innerHTML. The greeting contains your first name and your code.
  3. Your page writes to the console. That is three lines. Line 1 is your favourite subject. Line 2 is your favourite food. Line 3 is your code.
  4. Your program shows a window. The window is called alert(). It opens when the page loads. The text in the window contains your number.

Your two values — you need no notebook and no pen

  • Your code: your school ID + your initials, lower case, no dot. Example: 12345ms
  • Your number: the last digit of your school ID. Example: school ID 12345 → number 5

In the end, your code is in the page title. Your greeting is on the page. Your three lines are in the console. Your window shows your number. Your result is clearly yours — not the one of the person next to you.

Allowed tools

You only use what you learned in chapter 01. Everything else is forbidden.

Allowed

  • Output: console.log, alert, getElementById and innerHTML
  • Text (strings)
  • HTML: basic structure, heading, elements with id
  • CSS: colours, font size, spacing
  • Embedding: script in the head with defer

Not allowed

  • Variables (const, let) — they come in chapter 02
  • prompt() — that comes later
  • Own functions — they come in chapter 07
  • document.write() — that is taboo
  • Input fields and buttons with events — they come in chapters 09 and 10

If the AI sends you code with words from the “Not allowed” list — do not use it. Rather ask the AI: “Explain to me …”. You build it yourself.

Design guidelines

Your page is dark. Your code is in the page title. The greeting is easy to read.

Guidelines

  • Dark background, like the chapter pages
  • Your code in the page title, clearly visible
  • The greeting is readable: large font, good contrast
  • The page has a margin to the edge

Everything else you design freely. Surprise me.

Requirements

Tick every line only when your code really does it. At the end, print the list as a PDF.

Step by step

Six steps. Plan about 20 minutes. After each step, reload your page.

Step 1 · Create the folder

Create the folder uebung-01/. Inside it index.html, css/ and js/. Create the files css/style.css and js/main.js and link them in the HTML.

Step 2 · HTML skeleton

Build the basic structure from chapter 01. A heading h1 with your code. An element with id="gruss".

Step 3 · Set colours

Dark background and text colour in style.css. Check it in the browser.

Step 4 · Embed the script

<script src="js/main.js" defer></script> into the head. Test: the page loads without errors.

Step 5 · Three outputs

Write the greeting with innerHTML. Three console.log lines. One alert() window with your number.

Step 6 · Test

Reload the page. Open the console (F12). Check: greeting visible, three lines in the console, window with your number.

Work with the AI

The rule: the AI explains, you build yourself. Have code explained to you — not delivered. If you take code from the AI, you must be able to explain every line.

Your starter prompt

Three check questions

  1. What does defer do — and why does the script still go into the head?
  2. Where do you see the console.log lines — and how do you open the console?
  3. Why is document.write() taboo?
