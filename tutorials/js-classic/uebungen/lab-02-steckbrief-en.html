Lab 02 — Profile Card Generator

Build your profile card. Four questions, four variables, one page about you. Time: about 30 min.

Your task

You build a profile card about yourself. Your program asks four questions. Your answers land in variables. Your page shows the profile.

  1. Your page has three files. The files are called index.html, css/style.css and js/main.js. All three live in one folder and are linked.
  2. Your program asks four times. Each time you see an input window. The window is called prompt(). You type your answer into it. Each answer goes into a variable. A variable is a container with a name. The four questions are: What is your name? How old are you? What is your favourite subject? What is your hobby?
  3. Your page shows the profile. All four answers are on the page. Each answer is on its own line. The profile also contains your code and your number.
  4. Your program writes to the console. That is exactly one sentence. The sentence contains your first name and your age.

Your two values — you need no notebook and no pen

  • Your code: your school ID + your initials, lower case, no dot. Example: 12345ms
  • Your number: the last digit of your school ID. Example: school ID 12345 → number 5

In the end, your code is in the page title. Your profile is complete. Your result is clearly yours — not the one of the person next to you.

Allowed tools

You only use what you learned in chapters 01–02. Everything else is forbidden.

Allowed

  • Variables: const and let
  • Text and numbers (strings, numbers)
  • Input: prompt()
  • Building text: template literals (backticks ` and ${`{`}…})
  • Output: console.log, innerHTML, getElementById
  • HTML/CSS: basic structure, embedded with defer

Not allowed

  • if, else — they come in chapter 04
  • Loops (for, while) — they come in chapter 05
  • Own functions — they come in chapter 07
  • Arrays — they come in chapter 06
  • Input fields and buttons with events — they come in chapters 09 and 10

If the AI sends you code with words from the “Not allowed” list — do not use it. Rather ask the AI: “Explain to me …”. You build it yourself.

Design guidelines

Your page is dark. Your code is in the title. The profile looks like a card.

Guidelines

  • Dark background, like the chapter pages
  • Your code in the page title, clearly visible
  • The profile is an easy-to-read card: spacing, border or own background
  • Each of the four answers is on its own line

Everything else you design freely. Surprise me.

Requirements

Tick every line only when your code really does it. At the end, print the list as a PDF.

Step by step

Six steps. Plan about 20 minutes. After each step, reload your page.

Step 1 · Create the folder

Create the folder uebung-02/. Inside it index.html, css/ and js/. Create css/style.css and js/main.js and link them.

Step 2 · HTML skeleton

Heading h1 with your code. An element with id="steckbrief".

Step 3 · Style the card

Dark background in style.css. The element #steckbrief gets spacing, a border or its own background.

Step 4 · Four questions

Four prompt() calls. Store each answer in its own const variable.

Step 5 · Write the profile

A template literal builds the text: four lines, code, number. Write it into #steckbrief with innerHTML.

Step 6 · Console and test

Output the sentence with console.log. Reload the page, answer the four windows, check the page and the console.

Work with the AI

The rule: the AI explains, you build yourself. Have code explained to you — not delivered. If you take code from the AI, you must be able to explain every line.

Your starter prompt

Three check questions

  1. What is the difference between const and let — in one sentence?
  2. Why do your prompt() lines come before the innerHTML?
  3. What does ${`{`}…} do in a template literal?
