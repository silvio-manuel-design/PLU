Lab 06 — Shopping List Pro

Arrays collect your entries. You build a list that sorts and filters. Time: about 30 min.

Your task

You build a program. Your program is a shopping list. The program runs in four steps.

  1. Your array is filled with five items. An array is a list of values. The first item starts with your code. Example: your code is 12345ms. Then your first item is 12345ms-Apples. You choose the other four items yourself. For example: Bread, Milk, Eggs, Water5. Important: one of your items contains your number. In the example, this is Water5.
  2. Your program asks you three times. Each time you see an input window. The input window is called prompt(). You write an item into the window. The program adds the item to the list. After three times, the list has eight items.
  3. Your program sorts the list. The list is sorted from A to Z. The program writes the list onto the page. Between the items is an arrow. Example: 12345ms-Apples → Butter → Eggs → …
  4. Your program looks for your number. Your number is the last digit of your school ID. Example: your school ID ends with 5 — then your number is 5. You do not need to remember the number. It is inside your code. The program looks at every item in the list. Is your number in the item name? All items with your number go into their own line under the list. Example: Items with your number: Water5

Your two values — you need no notebook and no pen

  • Your code: your school ID + your initials, lowercase, no dot. Example: 12345ms
  • Your number: the last digit of your school ID. Example: school ID 12345 → number 5

At the end, your code is in the page title. Your list is sorted. Your number has its own line. Your result is clearly yours — not the one of the person next to you.

Allowed tools

You only use what you learned in chapters 01–06. Everything else is forbidden.

Allowed

  • Variables: const and let
  • Texts and numbers (strings, numbers)
  • Arrays: index, length, push, pop, unshift, shift, join, sort, includes, indexOf
  • Loops: for and for...of
  • Decisions: if, else if, else
  • Output: console.log, innerHTML, getElementById
  • Input: prompt()
  • Building texts: template literals

Not allowed

  • Your own functions — they come in chapter 07
  • filter and map — they come later
  • Arrow functions (=>)
  • Input fields and buttons with events — they come in chapters 09 and 10

If the AI sends you code with words from the "Not allowed" list — do not use it. Ask the AI instead: "Explain … to me." You build it yourself.

Design guidelines

Your page should be dark. Your code is in the page title. The shopping list is easy to read. The line with your number stands out.

Guidelines

  • Dark background, like the chapter pages
  • Your code in the page title, easy to see
  • The list is readable: enough space, clear font
  • The number line looks different from the list

Everything else you design freely. Surprise me.

Requirements

Tick every line when your code really does it. At the end, print the list as a PDF.

Step by step

Six steps. Plan about 20 minutes. After each step, reload your page.

Step 1 · Create the folder

Create the folder uebung-06/. Inside: index.html, css/ and js/. Create the files css/style.css and js/main.js and link them in the HTML.

Step 2 · Start the list

Array shopping with five items. The first one carries your code, one contains your number.

Step 3 · Ask

Call prompt() three times. Add every answer to the list with push.

Step 4 · Onto the page

Turn the list into one text with join(" → "). Write it onto the page with innerHTML.

Step 5 · Sort

Call sort(). Write the list onto the page one more time.

Step 6 · Number line

Go through the list with for. Look for your number with includes. Write the hits into their own line.
