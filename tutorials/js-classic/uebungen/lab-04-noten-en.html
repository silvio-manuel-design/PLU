Lab 04 — Grade Calculator

Build a grade evaluator. Points in, grade out. Time: about 30 min.

Your task

You build a grade evaluator. You enter your points. The program calculates the percentage and decides the grade.

  1. Your page has three files. The files are called index.html, css/style.css and js/main.js. All three live in one folder and are linked.
  2. Your program asks for your points. The question is: How many points did you reach? The answer comes from prompt() and becomes a number with Number(). The maximum points are in a const: 20 + your number. Example: your number is 5 — the maximum is 25.
  3. Your program calculates the percentage. Formula: reached points × 100 ÷ maximum points. The result is a number between 0 and 100.
  4. Your program decides the grade. With if, else if and else: percentage at least 90 → grade “Excellent", at least 75 → “Good", at least 60 → “Satisfactory", at least 50 → “Sufficient", otherwise → “Insufficient". Grade, percentage and one sentence are on the page. For “Insufficient" there is an extra sentence.

Your two values — you need no notebook and no pen

  • Your code: your school ID + your initials, lower case, no dot. Example: 12345ms
  • Your number: the last digit of your school ID — it sets the maximum points (20 + number). Example: school ID 12345 → maximum 25 points

In the end, your code is in the page title. Your maximum is your number. Your grade has five levels. Your result is clearly yours — not the one of the person next to you.

Allowed tools

You only use what you learned in chapters 01–04. Everything else is forbidden.

Allowed

  • Everything from Lab 01–03: const, let, prompt(), Number(), arithmetic, template literals, console.log, innerHTML
  • Decisions: if, else if, else
  • Comparisons: >=, <=, >, <, ===

Not allowed

  • Loops (for, while) — they come in chapter 05
  • switch — in this lab we stay with if and else
  • && and || — they come later
  • Own functions — they come in chapter 07
  • Arrays — they come in chapter 06

If the AI sends you code with words from the “Not allowed” list — do not use it. Rather ask the AI: “Explain to me …”. You build it yourself.

Design guidelines

Your page is dark. Your code is in the title. The result is the centre of attention.

Guidelines

  • Dark background, like the chapter pages
  • Your code in the page title, clearly visible
  • Percentage and grade are large and easy to read
  • Good grades (1 and 2) look different from bad grades (4 and 5) — for example by colour

Everything else you design freely. Surprise me.
