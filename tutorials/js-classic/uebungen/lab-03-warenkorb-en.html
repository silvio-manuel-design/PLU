Lab 03 — Cart Calculator

Build a mini shop. Prices, quantities, arithmetic in JavaScript. Time: about 30 min.

Your task

You build a mini shop. Your program calculates an order. Three products, two quantity questions, one bill.

  1. Your page has three files. The files are called index.html, css/style.css and js/main.js. All three live in one folder and are linked.
  2. Your shop has three products. Pencil 2 euros, notebook 5 euros, eraser 1 euro. The prices are in const variables. Shipping costs your number euros — that is a variable too.
  3. Your program asks two times. How many pencils? How many notebooks? The answers come from prompt() and are turned into numbers with Number().
  4. Your program calculates and shows the bill. Line 1: pencil price × quantity. Line 2: notebook price × quantity. Line 3: shipping. Line 4: the total. Every line is on the page. The total is also in the console.

Your two values — you need no notebook and no pen

  • Your code: your school ID + your initials, lower case, no dot. Example: 12345ms
  • Your number: the last digit of your school ID — it is your shipping price in euros. Example: school ID 12345 → shipping 5 euros

In the end, your code is in the page title. Your bill is complete. Your shipping price is your number. Your result is clearly yours — not the one of the person next to you.

Allowed tools

You only use what you learned in chapters 01–03. Everything else is forbidden.

Allowed

  • Everything from Lab 01 and Lab 02: const, let, prompt(), template literals, console.log, innerHTML
  • Arithmetic: +, -, *, /
  • Text to number: Number()

Not allowed

  • if, else — they come in chapter 04
  • Loops (for, while) — they come in chapter 05
  • Own functions — they come in chapter 07
  • Arrays — they come in chapter 06
  • toFixed() — with whole numbers you do not need it

If the AI sends you code with words from the “Not allowed” list — do not use it. Rather ask the AI: “Explain to me …”. You build it yourself.

Design guidelines

Your page is dark. Your code is in the title. The bill looks like a bill.

Guidelines

  • Dark background, like the chapter pages
  • Your code in the page title, clearly visible
  • The bill is an easy-to-read card
  • The total line stands out: bolder or larger than the other lines

Everything else you design freely. Surprise me.

Requirements

Tick every line only when your code really does it. At the end, print the list as a PDF.
