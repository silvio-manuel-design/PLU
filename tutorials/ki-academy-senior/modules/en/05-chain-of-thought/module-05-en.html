What is Chain of Thought?

Think like a Detective

Like Sherlock Holmes - Step by Step to the Result

The Detective Method

Imagine Sherlock Holmes. He doesn't solve cases through a sudden flash of insight, but through systematic, step-by-step thinking. He gathers clues, connects them logically, and arrives at the solution. That's exactly how Chain of Thought works.

A CoT Prompt in Action:

"Solve this math problem step by step. Explain each intermediate step and show how you arrive at the result."

The 5 Steps

The CoT Process at a Glance

Each step builds on the previous one. Click the cards for details.

0 of 5 steps discovered
1

Understand the Problem

What exactly needs to be solved?

"I want to find out whether buying a new car is worth it, given my budget and my driving needs..."
Aha!

Before you ask AI, ask yourself: What do I really want to know? The clearer your question, the more precise the answer. A retiree who wants "better photos" gets something different than one who wants to know about "focus at dusk."

2

Gather Information

What data and facts do we need?

"Consider purchase price, insurance, fuel costs, maintenance, and resale value..."
Aha!

AI can only work with what you give it. Imagine asking a friend for advice – you'd say: "My budget is €2,000, I don't like heights, and we're traveling in September." That's exactly how AI works.

3

Intermediate Results

What does each individual calculation yield?

"First calculate the monthly costs, then the annual total costs, compare with my budget..."
Aha!

Complex problems are like a puzzle. AI lays one piece after another. If you understand the intermediate step, you also understand the result. And if something seems odd, you can ask exactly there.

4

Connect

How do the results relate to each other?

"Compare the total costs over 5 years with my current car and alternative means of transport..."
Aha!

This is the magical moment! AI connects the individual puzzle pieces into a picture. As a senior, you have the advantage of life experience – you immediately recognize whether the overall picture fits or if a piece is missing.

5

Draw Conclusion

What is the final recommendation?

"Summarize the key points and give a clear recommendation with reasoning..."
Aha!

The conclusion is your decision aid, not your command! AI suggests, you decide. If the conclusion doesn't suit you, say: "I don't like that. What if we..." and start a new train of thought.

Wonderful! All steps discovered!

You've learned all 5 building blocks of the Chain of Thought process. You're ready for complex problems!

Compare a simple prompt with a Chain of Thought prompt

Your Prompt:

My doctor prescribed an antibiotic: 1 capsule 3 times daily for 8 days. The pack has 20 capsules. Do I need a second pack?
Without CoT

Answer: No, 20 capsules are enough.

Wrong! No calculation 20 feels like enough
With CoT
1

Given: 3 capsules/day, 8 days duration, pack = 20 capsules

2

Calculation: 3 × 8 = 24 capsules needed

3

Comparison: 24 needed > 20 available → Pack is not enough!

✓

Conclusion: Yes, a second pack is needed. Without calculation, 20 seems sufficient.

Solving a Math Problem

Aha!

A retiree with €50,000 in savings wants to know how much he can withdraw monthly. Instead of just naming the formula, AI shows every intermediate step: interest rate, annuity formula, substitution, result. This way you can TRACE whether it's correct.

Problem

A retiree has €50,000 in savings. He wants to know how much he can withdraw monthly at 3% interest over 10 years.

Step 1

Identify the variables: Capital = €50,000, Interest = 3%, Time period = 10 years

Step 2

Calculate the interest factor: 1 + (3/100) = 1.03 per year

Step 3

Apply the annuity formula: R = K × (q-1) / (1-q^-n)

Step 4

Calculate: R = 50,000 × 0.03 / (1-1.03^-10) ≈ €5,860 per year

Solution

Result: The retiree can withdraw approximately €488 per month without depleting the capital.

Planning a Trip on a Budget

Aha!

A couple (70+) plans an Italy vacation with a €3,000 budget. AI doesn't just compare prices, but considers ALL factors: comfort, accessibility, stress, food. The result: Lake Maggiore by night train – romantic AND relaxed.

Problem

A couple (70+) wants to take a 2-week vacation in Italy with a €3,000 budget. Which region? Flight or train?

Step 1

Analyze preferences: Relaxed, easily accessible, low stress, great food

Step 2

Compare transport: Train (relaxed, central) vs. Flight (faster, more transfers)

Step 3

Cost breakdown: Accommodation ~€1,400, Food ~€700, Transport ~€600, Activities ~€300

Step 4

Region check: Tuscany (good by car), Lakes (good by train), Sicily (far away)

Solution

Recommendation: Lake Maggiore by night train. Romantic, relaxed arrival, budget fits.

Making a Business Decision

Aha!

A craftsman (62) wonders: Online shop or second store? AI analyzes not just costs, but also quality of life: Online = flexible, but tech learning curve. Store = personal, but must be present. The result: Online shop with local delivery area – the best of both worlds.

Problem

Should I expand my small crafts business online or open an additional shop?

Step 1

Define goal: More revenue with minimal risk, suitable for age (62)

Step 2

Online analysis: Advantages (flexible, low fixed costs), Disadvantages (tech learning curve, competition)

Step 3

Shop analysis: Advantages (personal, proven), Disadvantages (high rent, must be present)

Step 4

Risk assessment: Online = scalable, Shop = higher capital risk

Solution

Recommendation: Start with an online shop with a local delivery area - low risk, personal connection remains.
