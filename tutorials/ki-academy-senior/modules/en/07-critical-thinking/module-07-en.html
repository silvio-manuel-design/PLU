Critical Thinking

The Detective Approach

Like Sherlock Holmes, we analyze information – before we accept it

The Case of the Distorted Truth

Imagine you are a detective. A case lands on your desk: An AI claims something. But as with any statement, you must ask: Who says this? How reliable is the source? Is there evidence?

The three questions of the critical thinker:
  • Who is the source? (Check authority)
  • What is being claimed? (Fact vs. opinion)
  • Why is it being said? (Understand intention)
Facts & Opinions

The Difference That Matters

AI often mixes facts with opinions – learn to distinguish both

Facts

  • Verifiable and documented
  • Data, statistics, measurements
  • Date, place, person – provable
Example: "Vienna is the capital of Austria."
VS

Opinions

  • Interpretation and evaluation
  • Subjective conviction
  • Cannot be wrong, only a different opinion
Example: "Vienna is the most beautiful city in the world."
Source Checking

The Digital Trace Search

How to systematically check the credibility of a source

01

WHOIS Check

Who operates the website? How long has it existed? Which country?

02

Check Date

Is the information current? Was it updated or archived?

03

Research Author

Who wrote it? What are their qualifications? What else have they published?

04

Cross-Reference

Are there other sources reporting the same thing? Do the details match?

"What can be asserted without evidence can also be dismissed without evidence."

— Christopher Hitchens

Hallucinations

When AI Hallucinates

AI sometimes invents facts – here's how you recognize misinformation

Invented Sources

AI often cites books, studies, or people that don't exist. Always check!

Wrong Data

Data, statistics, and facts can be completely made up. Never blindly trust.

Contradictory

In long texts, AI sometimes contradicts itself. Read carefully!

Case Studies

Reality Check

Typical situations and how to handle them

The Message in the Family Chat

A relative shares an article with shocking claims. What do you do?

Tip: Read first, then think. Check the source. If no serious source is recognizable – don't forward.

The AI Makes a Specific Claim

The AI gives a specific number or date. Can that be right?

Tip: AI has no "knowledge" – it generates probable texts. Always verify facts externally.
