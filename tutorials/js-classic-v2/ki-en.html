The three rules

1

Understand it yourself first, then ask the AI

If you know nothing, you can't judge the answer. Read first, ask second.

2

Check every AI answer

In the course, in the playground or on MDN. Confidently wrong is the most common error type.

3

Rewrite it in your own words

Rewriting AI answers IS the learning. If you copy, you learn nothing.

The five fundamentals of prompting

Five moves that turn an arbitrary answer into a useful one. They come in this order — from role to system prompt.

  1. 1

    Role

    Give the AI a role — it plays it seriously. „You are my patient JavaScript coach for beginners. Explain to me …" — one clear role sets level, tone and expectation in a single sentence.

  2. 2

    Context

    Provide everything the AI needs: your level, the goal, the code, the error message. The more context, the better the answer. „It doesn't work" helps no one — the full error text does.

  3. 3

    Iteration

    The first prompt is rarely the best. Asking follow-ups is not a weakness: „Shorter." / „More precisely." / „Show me a counterexample." You negotiate your way to a good answer, step by step.

  4. 4

    Chain of Thought

    Ask for verifiable intermediate steps: „Explain your reasoning step by step and show your calculations and intermediate results before you give the answer." That way you can follow each step and see where the AI takes a wrong turn — you learn more than from the finished answer. (AIs don't always show their complete internal thinking — verifiable steps are what you actually need.)

  5. 5

    System prompts

    Set ground rules for the whole chat: „Always answer with an everyday example." / „Check your answer yourself first." Whatever stands as a system prompt, you don't have to rewrite in every prompt. Different from that: custom instructions in your profile — those apply across all chats, while a system prompt only applies to the single one.

Three of these terms are ready to read in the PLU lexicon: Chain of Thought, System Prompt and Prompt.

Good prompts, bad prompts

Bad: „do the task"

No goal, no context — the answer will be arbitrary.

Good: „Show me an example of a for loop with comments, then explain line 3"

Bad: „explain code"

Which code? Which level? The AI has to guess.

Good: „Explain this function line by line, as if I were a beginner: [paste code]"

Bad: „it doesn't work"

Without error message and code, nobody can help.

Good: „This code throws ‚x is not defined' — line 4. What's the cause? [paste code + error]"

How to spot AI mistakes

Warning signs: overly general answers, outdated syntax, invented methods, missing brackets. Antidote: test in the playground and cross-check on MDN — two minutes that clear up every self-doubt.

AI & school

Homework honesty: the reflections in this course have cheat protection — platitudes don't count. The basic rule is simple:

  • Allowed: AI as a learning partner — ask, have things explained, have answers checked.
  • Not allowed: AI as a replacement — submitting something you haven't understood.
  • Declare: with every submission, two or three sentences: „What did I work with, where did the AI help?" That's not a punishment — it shows that you think.

Your teacher's rule always applies. And the responsibility for the result lies with you: the AI doesn't get a grade — you do.
