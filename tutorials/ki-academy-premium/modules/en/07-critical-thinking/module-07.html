The 4 Steps

Critical Thinking with AI

A structured approach to evaluating AI output. Follow these steps and turn blind trust into informed judgment.

01

Question Assumptions

What does the AI take for granted? Every answer rests on invisible premises. Make them visible before you act.

02

Check Sources

Where does the knowledge come from? AI blends training data without citations. Verify facts that matter.

03

Explore Counter-Positions

What speaks against it? The best way to test an idea is to try to destroy it. Ask the AI to play devil's advocate.

04

Test Consistency

Same answer with different prompts? Rephrase and re-ask. Inconsistent answers signal uncertainty or hallucination.

Toolbox

Your Critical Thinking Toolkit

These five tools transform you from a passive consumer into an active evaluator of AI output.

1

Fact-Check

Verify accuracy. Cross-check dates, names, statistics, and claims with reliable sources. AI can be confidently wrong.

2

Bias Detection

Identify distortions. AI reflects the biases in its training data. Watch for stereotypes, skewed framing, and missing perspectives.

3

Source Analysis

Evaluate origin. Ask where the information comes from. AI rarely cites sources — demand transparency for important decisions.

4

Logic Check

Find contradictions. Follow the argument step by step. Does A really lead to B? Are there gaps or fallacies?

5

Consistency Test

Self-correction. Rephrase your question and compare answers. Consistent responses are more likely to be reliable.

Live Example

From Analysis to Cross-Check

Four steps – from first assessment to verified insight. The cross-check across multiple AIs is the most important step.

Round 1: Initial Analysis
Human
Analyze the claim: "Remote work increases productivity." Give me a balanced view with pros and cons.
AI A (ChatGPT)
Balanced Analysis: Remote Work & Productivity

Pros: Fewer commutes mean more focused hours. Many workers report higher satisfaction and lower burnout. Access to global talent pools increases team capability.

Cons: Collaboration can suffer without spontaneous in-person interaction. Onboarding new employees is harder remotely. Some roles require physical presence and cannot be evaluated by the same metric.

Verdict: The effect depends heavily on role type, company culture, and measurement criteria.
Round 2: Devil's Advocate
Human
Now play devil's advocate. Critique your own analysis. What did you miss? What assumptions did you make?
AI A (ChatGPT)
Self-Critique & Missing Angles:

Assumption 1: I assumed "productivity" is universally defined. In reality, output per hour, creative breakthroughs, and team cohesion are very different metrics — and remote work affects each differently.

Missed angle: The selection bias in studies. Companies that adopt remote work successfully may already have strong management cultures. The causality may be reversed.

Missed angle: Long-term career effects. Junior employees learn faster through osmosis in offices. Remote work may trade short-term output for long-term skill development gaps.

Weakness: I presented the verdict as balanced, but I did not quantify how often or for whom each scenario applies.
Round 3: Consistency Check
Human
Rephrase: Does working from home make people more efficient? Compare with your previous answer.
AI A (ChatGPT)
Consistency Check:

Core claim holds: It depends. The nuanced verdict is consistent across both phrasings.

Refinement: Your rephrasing highlights "efficiency" rather than "productivity." Efficiency emphasizes output per hour — where remote work often wins. Productivity is broader and includes innovation, which may suffer remotely.

Self-correction: My first answer underweighted the measurement problem. Your rephrasing made it clearer that the question itself is ambiguous without defining the metric.

Conclusion: The answer is consistent but the second framing sharpened the focus on what actually matters — how you measure success.
Round 4: Cross-Check Across AIs ⭐ ESSENTIAL
Human
The same original question is now asked to a different AI. Compare the answers — where do they agree? Where do they contradict? What does AI B uncover that AI A missed?
AI B (Claude)
Comparative Analysis of AI Responses:

Agreement: Both AIs emphasize context-dependency. Both mention commute time and flexibility as pro arguments.

Contradiction: AI A was more optimistic about remote work; AI B emphasized the innovation gap and "Zoom fatigue" more strongly. AI B cited specific figures (15-25% productivity gains in studies), which AI A omitted.

What AI B uncovered:
• The "productivity illusion" — many remote workers work longer, not more efficiently
• The mentorship loss — junior employees learn slower without direct contact
• The asymmetric distribution — remote work favors certain personality types

➜ Bottom Line: No single AI delivers the whole truth. Cross-checking across different AIs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity) provides the most reliable path to a complete picture. Each AI has its own training data, blind spots, and strengths.

The Most Important Step: Cross-Check

An AI doesn't intentionally lie — but it can be wrong, produce hallucinations, or miss important aspects. The same prompt across ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Perplexity yields four different perspectives. Where they all agree is likely correct. Where they contradict, the truth lies in the middle — or there is no clear answer at all. Cross-checking is not optional; it is mandatory for important decisions.

What You Take With You

Question Everything

Assumptions, sources, and conclusions. The best AI users are the ones who never stop asking "How do you know?"

Seek Counter-Positions

The strongest ideas survive the strongest objections. Make devil's advocacy a habit, not an afterthought.

Test for Consistency

Rephrase and re-ask. Consistent answers are more reliable. Inconsistency is a red flag worth investigating.
